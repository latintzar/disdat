The tools for showing your agent what you mean.

Peter A NeyraFree & open source · 5 min read

There's a small family of tools that help an AI coding agent see and act on what's on your screen. Most are genuinely good. Here's the honest map — what each is great at, where to get it, and where Dis Dat fits. Use whatever serves you; I mean that.

Inspect & ground truth

Chrome DevTools

The original source of truth — inspect any element, read computed styles, the box model, the network. Pair it with chrome-devtools-mcp to hand that to an agent. Free, built into the browser.

Point at your own app, let your editor agent fix it

Stagewise

A toolbar that lives inside your dev app — click an element, comment on it, and your Cursor/Windsurf agent edits the source. Brilliant when the thing you're fixing is your own running project. Open source.

Let an agent drive the browser for you

Browser Use

Hand an agent the wheel — it navigates, clicks and fills out whole tasks across the web on its own. A different job entirely: automation, not pointing. Open source.

Claude for Chrome

Claude working alongside you, right in the browser — reading the page and taking actions. Great as a general in-browser assistant.

So where does Dis Dat fit?

Those tools either drive the page, inspect it by hand, or copy a chunk of it. Dis Dat does a smaller, different thing: you point or talk at anything on your screen — your localhost, a live site, anyone's — and it works out which element you meant, reads the real render beneath it (computed styles, DOM, state, network, draw calls), and hands your agent the exact handle, in plain code-speak.

It doesn't try to be your agent, or wire into your repo, or automate tasks. It's the capture layer for the agent you already drive — Cursor, Claude Code, Codex. The bit none of the others do: turning what you're reacting to into something your agent can act on, repo or not.

If a tool above fits your job better, use it. If you keep wishing you could just point and say it — that's us.
